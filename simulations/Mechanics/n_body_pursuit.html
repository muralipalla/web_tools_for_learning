N people stand at the vertices of a regular polygon. At the same instant, each runs at speed v directly toward the next person. No one aims at the centre—so what path will they trace, and will the chase ever end?

Resolve the chase

A constant inward component guarantees a meeting.

The direction to the next person always makes the same angle with the inward radius. Every velocity therefore splits into an unchanging radial speed and a tangential speed. The inward part closes the radius in finite time; the sideways part bends every path into a logarithmic spiral.

01

Symmetry survives

Rotating the whole scene by 2π/N swaps every person with the next. So the group must remain a smaller, rotated regular polygon throughout the chase.

02

Split the velocity

With α = π/N, the inward radial component is v sin α, while the tangential component is v cos α. Turn on Velocity components to compare the two components directly on person 1.

03

Radius falls steadily

Because the inward speed is constant, R(t) = R0 − v sin(π/N)t. The radius reaches zero instead of merely approaching it.

Meeting time T = R0v sin(π/N)

Faster runners meet sooner. More people make the inward component smaller, so the chase lasts longer.

Path length each person runs L = vT = R0sin(π/N)

The speed cancels: changing v changes the duration, not the length or shape of the path.

Path equation R = R0 e−tan(π/N)Δθ

Here Δθ is the angle swept from each person’s own starting radius. This is a logarithmic spiral. Near the meeting point the group turns ever faster while its radius becomes ever smaller—an infinite amount of turning squeezed into a finite time and distance.

Model rules: all people start simultaneously at the vertices of a perfect regular polygon, move at constant speed, turn continuously with no delay, and always pursue the next person in the same direction around the polygon. The initial circumradius is fixed at 10 m.
