Call it before it rolls

The classic 3 : 1 challenge

Take the fixed radius as R = 1 and the rolling radius as r = ⅓. How many complete turns occur while the moving centre completes one orbit?

Both answers have an argument. Which visualization will confirm the count?

Tempting solution 3 turns

R / r = 1 / ⅓ = 3

This counts rolling against the rim but forgets that the line of centres turns once.
Correct solution 4 turns

R / r + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4

The orientation cue makes four complete revolutions in the room’s frame.

Unroll the surprise

The path is larger than the fixed circle.

The roller’s centre stays one roller radius beyond the fixed rim. That tiny offset lasts all the way around—and becomes exactly one extra roller circumference.

01

The tempting count

Comparing the two circumferences gives R / r. For R = 1 and r = ⅓, that predicts three turns.

02

Follow the centre

Its orbit has radius R + r, so the distance travelled is 2π(R + r), not 2πR.

03

The extra turn

Dividing that centre-path distance by the roller circumference adds exactly +1.
