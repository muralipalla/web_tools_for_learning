The thought-experiment rules

  • 01A narrow, perfectly smooth vacuum tube keeps the ball on the chord.
  • 02Earth does not rotate.
  • 03Density is uniform, so the maths stays beautifully simple.
  • 04In a wide angled hole, the ball would curve toward Earth’s centre and hit the wall.

Unpack the surprise

Gravity fades. Momentum carries on.

Inside a uniform Earth, only the mass closer to the centre than the ball pulls it inward overall. The deeper it falls, the weaker that inward pull becomes.

01

At the surface

The ball starts from rest. Gravity is strongest here and points toward the centre.

02

At the tunnel midpoint

The pull along the tube is zero, but speed is greatest. On a tilted path, gravity still pulls sideways and the smooth tube pushes back.

03

At the far exit

It slows to a momentary stop, then falls back. With no drag, the cosmic commute repeats forever.

The along-tunnel rule a∥(s) = −(g/R)s

Here s is distance from the chord’s midpoint. The same simple-harmonic rule survives every angle: midpoint in about 21 minutes, the other end in about 42 minutes—even when the tunnel is shorter.

Reality check: Earth’s heat, pressure, rotation, changing density, and atmosphere make this tunnel impossible. That is exactly why physicists love thought experiments: remove the mess, reveal the idea.
