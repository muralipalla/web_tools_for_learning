Hydrogen Atomic Spectrum from a Diffraction Grating

This simulation generates hydrogen wavelengths from the Rydberg formula, sends them through a diffraction grating using d sin θ = mλ, and draws the separated spectral lines on a virtual screen. Switch on the magnetic field to see a simplified Zeeman splitting of the spectral lines.

Wavelength spectrum

The top panel shows grating separation on a screen. The lower panel shows the same lines on a wavelength axis. Balmer lines are visible; Lyman is ultraviolet and Paschen/Brackett are infrared.

Calculated spectral lines

Rydberg formula: 1/λ = RH(1/nf2 − 1/ni2); grating equation: d sin θ = mλ.

Simplified Zeeman model used here: each line becomes a triplet λ₀ − Δλ, λ₀, λ₀ + Δλ, where Δν = eB/(4πme) and Δλ ≈ λ²Δν/c. This is the normal-Zeeman picture; real hydrogen fine structure and anomalous Zeeman details are more subtle.
