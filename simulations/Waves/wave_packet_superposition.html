Wave Packets/ Wave Particle Duality

This simulation shows how a localized wave packet is formed by superposing many travelling waves with nearby wave numbers. Each individual wave is spread throughout space, but their combination can produce a particle-like localized pulse. By changing the number of waves, bandwidth, dispersion, and phase speed, you can explore wave-particle duality and the basic idea behind the uncertainty principle: increasing wave-number spread localizes the packet, while decreasing it makes the packet more spread out.

Concept

ψ(x,t) = Σ A(k) cos(kx − ω(k)t), where A(k) is peaked around k₀.

When all waves have nearly the same wave number, their sum is spread out. When many different nearby wave numbers are added, constructive interference occurs only in a limited region, forming a packet. If the medium is dispersive, different k components travel at different speeds and the packet can change shape.
